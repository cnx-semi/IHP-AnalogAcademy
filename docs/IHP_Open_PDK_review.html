The CML divider of section 3a was simulated from 2.5 V with every device below 1.6 V; from the 3.3 V domain it needs a regulator or dropper
  • Only two MOS levels. There is no 1.8 V or 2.5 V device. Other levels mean a thick-oxide device run at reduced voltage, or a regulator.
  • Two power domains and level shifters are unavoidable once a block uses both oxides, e.g. a 1.2 V SAR logic driving a 3.3 V bootstrapped switch. Each crossing adds delay and a place for timing skew.
  • Do not stack gate voltages across oxides. A 1.2 V device must not see more than its rated gate voltage; keep the thick/thin boundary at a level shifter, never on a shared node.
  • Supply noise. The 3.3 V analog supply and the 1.2 V digital supply should be separate on the board and in the package; clock-path jitter budgets (section 3) assume a quiet supply.

What is missing. The PDK has no ready ADC, DAC, PLL, oscillator or reference cells; those come from the course or from our own design. It also has no dedicated high-speed comparator, bootstrapped switch or current-steering cell, so the AnalogAcademy Module 3 versions are the starting point. The RF and HBT devices are characterised in section 3a; the open items are in section 6.

Numbers reconciled by measurement. The course README quotes HBT fT 350 GHz and fmax 500 GHz; adc/docs/03-silicon.html says fT 300 GHz. In the PDK's own model (typical corner, 27 °C, pre-layout) npn13G2 reaches fT 415 GHz and fmax 358 GHz at its best bias (about 2.5 mA per emitter, VCE = 1.0 V); at the worst corner and 125 °C it is 287 / 242 GHz. So 300–350 GHz is a fair design number for fT; the 500 GHz fmax headline is not reproduced by the model, use 240–360 GHz. The 720 GHz fmax on IHP's site is a different, experimental technology.

3b. Conservative ceilings for design

Method: the ceiling is where a first-pass design by a team new to this PDK should still work with margin: the measured worst-case value of 3a, reduced by about 40 % for layout wiring and pads where only pre-layout data exist. For HBT gain stages that is 35 GHz, where the transistor still has 18 dB of gain at the worst corner (U = 18 dB, 22 dB typical; 15 / 19 dB at 50 GHz). 3c. Pad and bond wire

What a TopMetal2 pad over a ground plane (the larger capacitance: 25 fF, 0.00 mS at 24 GHz; 25 fF, 0.04 mS at 77 GHz, section 3a) and a 25 µm gold bond wire do to a 50 Ω line (0.8 nH per mm; a QFN wire is 1–2 mm). The wire is a series inductor, the pad a shunt capacitor. “Compensated” adds a shunt capacitor on the package side, a series capacitor (at least 30 fF) and a shunt capacitor on the chip, chosen to hold the match over ±15 % wire length and ±2 % frequency. No leadframe, PCB pad or neighbouring-wire coupling is included (no vendor model): this bounds the interface, it is not a package model.

  • 24 GHz works with a QFN wire, but never as a plain 50 Ω connection: a 0.8 nH wire gives S11 −2.5 dB and 3.6 dB of mismatch loss. With a single-frequency MIM match the interface stays below −10 dB across a ±15 % wire-length spread up to about 1.2 nH, with 0.1 dB of loss.
  • 77 GHz does not work with an ordinary wire: 0.4 nH is 194 Ω of reactance, and no realisable capacitor combination restores a 50 Ω match. A flip-chip bump (about 50 pH), a 0.2 mm wire, on-wafer probing, or an antenna-in-package that avoids the interface are the options.
  • Do not put the PDK's ESD I/O cell in an RF path: 67 fF and 3 mS at 24 GHz (0.9 dB), 63 fF and 5 mS at 77 GHz (2.7 dB). A 2 kV diode pair adds 52 fF; at 77 GHz even that costs 3 dB, so a 77 GHz pin needs no ESD device or a custom one with a few fF.

Consequence for D1. The current ADC/DAC target of 200 MS/s digitises about 200 MHz of OFDM bandwidth, which is short of both cases: 250 MHz needs about 250 MS/s and 500 MHz needs about 500 MS/s (or 2.5× the planned interleaving). This holds for an OFDM waveform, where the converter must carry the full signal bandwidth. An FMCW (chirp) radar mixes the received signal against the transmitted chirp first, so its converter only sees a low beat frequency and 200 MS/s is plenty. The waveform choice therefore sets the converter rate, and the ADC/DAC rate should be fixed only after D1 names it.

  • Carrier at 24 GHz needs the HBT or a multiplier. The CMOS transistors are too slow for a 24 GHz oscillator or divider (LV NMOS fT 27–33 GHz at moderate inversion, TSPC divider 5–8 GHz). Either use an HBT oscillator at 24–26 GHz, or a CMOS oscillator near 12 GHz followed by a frequency doubler. The HBT oscillator was simulated with an EM inductor (section 3a: 23.0–24.9 GHz, −99 dBc/Hz at 1 MHz); the CMOS-plus-doubler option was not.
  • Bandwidth is regulatory as much as technical. 250 MHz is inside the ISM band; 500 MHz is not, so case B depends on the old wideband allocation or a test licence.
  • Plan A/B as one design: build for 250 MHz first and keep the waveform generator, DAC and ADC able to scale to 500 MHz.

Comments

  • The spec decides everything (D1). With carrier, IF, bandwidth and resolution undefined, none of this table is a design commitment. Add a carrier-frequency question to D1: does the chip only digitise a baseband or IF signal, or does it generate and receive the carrier?
  • If baseband only: the whole chip runs in 1.2 V CMOS, and the HBT advantage mostly goes unused. IHP is then chosen for its passives, mismatch libraries, free shuttle and the AnalogAcademy course, not for speed. The existing docs argue IHP is technically superior at ≥200 MS/s; that argument is weaker if everything stays CMOS.
  • If the carrier is on chip: the PLL (VCO plus first dividers) becomes the one block that truly needs HBTs, and it is a larger risk than the whole ADC. The AnalogAcademy course has no HBT or VCO module. Module 2 (the 50 GHz amplifier) is the only RF-flow practice and would move from “skip” to “useful”.
  • The DAC is the tightest block at 200 MS/s: usable output is capped near 65 MHz by the fs/3 rule. If the radar waveform needs more bandwidth, raise the DAC rate, not the architecture.
  • Clock jitter matters more than device speed. A 1.6 ps rms budget at 100 MHz input is demanding for a ring VCO; an LC VCO or an off-chip clean reference is the conservative choice.
  • 77 GHz in numbers. One transistor has 15 dB of unilateral gain at 77 GHz typical and 11 dB at the worst corner before matching and wiring; the course layout takes 1 dB of U and 0.2–0.3 dB of MAG away, pads and matching more. NFmin is 2.0 dB typical and 3.3 dB hot, 2.7 dB with a plain 50 Ω source (8 emitters). That is workable for a multi-stage LNA but leaves little margin for a power amplifier, and the package cannot be plastic QFN with wire bonds (section 3c). 24 GHz has 22–25 dB of device gain and 0.8–1.4 dB NFmin: roughly 10 dB more gain and 1–2 dB less noise.
  • VCO at 24 GHz, measured with an EM inductor. A single-turn TopMetal2 ring of 210 µm (0.42 nH differential, Q 44 at 24 GHz) replaces the earlier ideal 0.25 nH inductors. With 22 PDK varactor units per node the cross-coupled HBT oscillator (4 mA from 1.2 V) tunes 20.3–27.6 GHz at −98 dBc/Hz at 1 MHz (FoM 179 dBc/Hz). The radar only needs 1–2 %: 6 varactor units plus a 117 fF MIM per node give 23.0–24.9 GHz at −99 dBc/Hz, 22.9–25.0 GHz at 125 °C. The inductor is not the limit (Q 30 instead of 44 costs 0.3 dB); the transistors’ shot noise and the tail are. The method (impulse-sensitivity functions from ngspice transients) matches the closed form for an ideal LC oscillator within 0.3 dB; flicker noise is not included, so expect worse below a few 100 kHz.
  • VCO at 77 GHz, measured with an EM inductor. A 100 µm single-turn ring (0.19 nH, Q 40–75 in the runs, 57 at the finer mesh) and 2 varactor units per node tune 75.3–81.5 GHz typical, but 70.7–78.5 GHz at 125 °C and the slow corner: a 6 % drift, so the 8 % range cannot cover both the 5 % wide 76–81 GHz band and the corner spread: a coarse capacitor bank or calibration is needed. Phase noise is −84 dBc/Hz at 1 MHz (FoM 175 dBc/Hz), 14 dB worse than at 24 GHz, 10 dB of it from frequency scaling. A ~38 GHz oscillator with a doubler would by frequency scaling alone be about 4 dB better (not simulated).
  • Noise figure is bias-flat and size-independent. NFmin is within 0.05 dB for 1 to 8 emitters at 0.15–0.45 mA per emitter; more emitters only lower Rn (200 → 26 Ω) and the optimum source impedance, which at 77 GHz with 8 emitters is 46 + j49 Ω, nearly 50 Ω. Take the current from the gain stage, not from the noise: at the peak-fT current NFmin is 0.5–0.7 dB worse. These are model values; the PDK noise model was not checked against measurement here.
  • Supplies: digital and comparator at 1.2 V; sampling switch, bias and anything needing signal swing at 3.3 V; pads at 3.3 V. This forces two power domains and level shifters. HBT stacks (a CML divider needs three levels) were simulated from 2.5 V, each device below 1.6 V, so plan a 2.5 V regulator or dropper from the 3.3 V domain.
  • Anti-alias and reconstruction filters: the conservative choice is off-chip, or a gm-C filter at ~100 MHz. A fully on-chip active-RC filter at 100 MHz is beyond the ~20 MHz ceiling above.
